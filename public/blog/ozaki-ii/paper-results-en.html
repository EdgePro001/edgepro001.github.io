1 The Ozaki II pipeline and the conditions for INT8 computation

Ozaki II scales floating point inputs into integer values, represents each integer through sss small residues, computes the residue matrix products with sss INT8 GEMMs, and combines the results before restoring the original scale. 1.2 Why the modulus limit is 256

INT8 represents 256 values in [−128,127][-128,127][−128,127]. Conventional nonnegative residues lie in [0,mt−1][0,m_t-1][0,mt​−1]; for moduli larger than 128, some of them would not fit in signed INT8. Ozaki II uses centered residues:

ρm(x)∈[−⌊m2⌋, ⌈m2⌉−1].\rho_m(x)\in \left[-\left\lfloor\frac m2\right\rfloor,\, \left\lceil\frac m2\right\rceil-1\right].ρm​(x)∈[−⌊2m​⌋,⌈2m​⌉−1].

Subtracting a modulus preserves the represented congruence. For 2≤mt≤2562\le m_t\le2562≤mt​≤256, this entire interval fits in INT8. At the boundary mt=256m_t=256mt​=256, it is exactly [−128,127][-128,127][−128,127], with the nonnegative residue 128 represented by the congruent value −128.

What is preserved here is the input's information modulo the selected modulus. The original integer a′a'a′ can be far larger than the INT8 range; each channel stores only one of its residues. Multiple moduli jointly represent the required information, so the complete integer a′a'a′ does not have to fit into eight bits.

The moduli must also be pairwise coprime. Subject to this constraint, a larger modulus contributes more range bits, log⁡2mt\log_2m_tlog2​mt​, helping reach the required total budget with fewer GEMMs. Paper, Section 3.1

1.3 The dot product length determines whether INT32 accumulation is safe

After fitting the inputs, we must prevent overflow during multiplication and accumulation. For the ttt-th modulus, one output is

(Pt)ij=∑ℓ=1q(At)iℓ(Bt)ℓj.(P_t)_{ij}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}(A_t)_{i\ell}(B_t)_{\ell j}.(Pt​)ij​=ℓ=1∑q​(At​)iℓ​(Bt​)ℓj​.

The largest absolute INT8 value is 128, so an individual product has magnitude at most 1282=214128^2=2^{14}1282=214. Signed INT32 represents [−231,231−1][-2^{31},2^{31}-1][−231,231−1]. Summing absolute values gives a conservative bound independent of cancellation:

∣(Pt)ij∣≤∑ℓ=1q∣(At)iℓ(Bt)ℓj∣≤q 214.\left|(P_t)_{ij}\right| \le\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}\left|(A_t)_{i\ell}(B_t)_{\ell j}\right| \le q\,2^{14}.∣(Pt​)ij​∣≤ℓ=1∑q​∣(At​)iℓ​(Bt​)ℓj​∣≤q214.

The condition

q 214<231⟺q<217=131072,q\,2^{14}<2^{31} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q<2^{17}=131072,q214<231⟺q<217=131072,

therefore prevents overflow in the final result. Every intermediate partial sum is bounded by the same total of absolute values, so the guarantee does not rely on eventual cancellation and also covers different accumulation orders.

When integer products and sums stay within range, they are exact; they do not suffer from floating point significand rounding. This is the reason for using INT8 × INT8 → INT32: narrow inputs reduce hardware cost, while a wider accumulator preserves the dot product.

The strict inequality matters. At q=217q=2^{17}q=217, the worst-case bound reaches 2312^{31}231, exceeding the largest positive INT32 value. This is a sufficient condition. Longer dot products do not always overflow, but the general guarantee no longer holds.

For larger qqq, the inner dimension can be partitioned into blocks with safe accumulations. Reduce each block result modulo the selected modulus, then combine those residues within a safe range. Overflow must be prevented before it occurs; applying a remainder operation after an INT32 overflow does not generally recover the correct residue. Paper, Section 3.1

1.4 How many GEMMs are required

Each modulus contributes a pair of residue matrices and one main GEMM, so sss moduli require sss main GEMMs. Each channel uses its own INT32 accumulation; increasing sss does not change the preceding dot product length into sqsqsq.

The choice of sss depends on the required total range budget:

log⁡2M=∑t=1slog⁡2mt.\log_2 M=\sum_{t=1}^{s}\log_2m_t.log2​M=t=1∑s​log2​mt​.

A modulus close to 256 contributes almost eight bits. The paper's 16-modulus configuration supplies about 125 range bits in total, shared by the two inputs, accumulation headroom, and the signed range. With mild magnitude variation and suitable scaling and reconstruction precision, 16 main GEMMs can therefore plausibly support FP64-like accuracy. It is not a fixed count guaranteed to work for arbitrary inputs.

The suffix in fast-sss and accu-sss denotes the number of moduli used for the main products. Accu also performs a bound-estimation GEMM, which belongs to preprocessing.

2 Why constrain scaled entries by 2k2^k2k

Bounding inputs provides a sufficient bound on their product. If ∣a′∣≤2kA|a'|\le2^{k_A}∣a′∣≤2kA​ and ∣b′∣≤2kB|b'|\le2^{k_B}∣b′∣≤2kB​, then

(∣A′∣∣B′∣)ij≤q2kA+kB.\bigl(|A'||B'|\bigr)_{ij}\le q2^{k_A+k_B}.(∣A′∣∣B′∣)ij​≤q2kA​+kB​.

This lets us bound every output before computing the product. We also want scaling to be as large as possible: the integer grid corresponds to a step of 1/Dii1/D_{ii}1/Dii​ or 1/Ejj1/E_{jj}1/Ejj​ in the original scale. A larger scale generally loses less information during truncation.

For equal budgets on both inputs, Equation (18) in the paper is

k=⌊12log⁡2M/2−1q⌋.k=\left\lfloor\frac12\log_2\frac{M/2-1}{q}\right\rfloor.k=⌊21​log2​qM/2−1​⌋.

Expanding the product of moduli gives

k=⌊12(∑tlog⁡2mt−log⁡2q−1+log⁡2(1−2/M))⌋.k=\left\lfloor\frac12\left( \sum_t\log_2m_t-\log_2q-1+\log_2(1-2/M) \right)\right\rfloor.k=⌊21​(t∑​log2​mt​−log2​q−1+log2​(1−2/M))⌋.

Ignoring the floor and the small final correction explains the trend:

k≈12(∑tlog⁡2mt−log⁡2q−1).k\approx\frac12\left(\sum_t\log_2m_t-\log_2q-1\right).k≈21​(t∑​log2​mt​−log2​q−1).

The moduli supply range bits. The dot product length consumes accumulation headroom, the signed interval costs one bit, and the two inputs share the remaining budget. Adding a modulus close to eight bits contributes roughly four bits per input; multiplying qqq by four costs roughly one bit per input.

Paper Figure 3. Source: Section 3.1. This is a fixed point range budget, not a guarantee that every element retains the same number of floating point significant bits.

This formula connects the modulus count to the input budget, but shared scaling remains sensitive to magnitude variation. Large and small entries in the same row can cause the small ones to be truncated away. A bound that better reflects the actual product can reduce unnecessarily conservative scaling.

GH200 has strong native FP64 performance. For small matrices, OS II's extra work outweighs the benefit. At size 16384, fast-14 reaches 80.2/60.9≈1.32×80.2/60.9\approx1.32\times80.2/60.9≈1.32×. The hardware's throughput ratio between low and high precision matters considerably.

For every matched size and modulus count in these tables, fast is faster than accu. Increasing the modulus count lowers throughput for both. However, choosing a method requires comparing time to satisfy the same error target. If accu reaches that target with fewer moduli, the ranking at equal sss is insufficient. The throughput tables alone do not establish accuracy thresholds for each input; a practical configuration still needs validation on the target data.
